The app and its goals

Shura wa Istehsan is an open archive of hadith across four traditions: Ibadi, Imami, Sunni and Zaydi. The goal is a complete view of every chain of transmission behind a report, and of all of that report's tellings, across works and across the four traditions. Most hadith sites show a text and a grade. This one is meant to show how a report reached us: who narrated it from whom, in which work and tradition each telling appears, what is known about each transmitter, and what scholars have said.

The historical context is part of that view. For each transmitter the archive is meant to show when and where they lived and which eras and events they lived through, so that a chain can be read against its time. That context is the least developed part of the data today (see the limitations below).

It is built for serious readers who do not have a seminary library: practising Muslims who want to understand why a report is graded as it is, self-taught students, teachers who need sourced material, and researchers.

What a reader can do today

  • Read a report in Arabic with its source work, and with English where a provider supplied it and it is cleared for display.
  • See the chains behind it, branch by branch, with each name as written. For a name, every narrator the archive considers a possible match is shown with where that match comes from; only a match confirmed by the Arabic text, or decided by a reviewer, is marked verified.
  • Open a narrator to see what the lexicon claims about them (labelled as unverified), the teachers and students seen in verified links, assessments by named critics, and, once entered, events during their lifetime.
  • See gradings as each tradition gives them. A Sunni verdict and a Zaydi verdict on the same narrator sit side by side; neither overrides the other.
  • Read commentary and compare with other scriptures. Commentary and parallels in Jewish, Christian and other sources are catalogued with the classical Isra'iliyyat classification; both start as proposed and are shown only once published by a named curator.

What the goal needs that is not yet built

  • All tellings of one report, across works and traditions. A study has found candidate links between reports; the tables, the loader and the reader's view are designed but not built.
  • Identities for Imami, Ibadi and Zaydi chains. Verified identities exist only for Sunni chains today.
  • Reading a work in order, with previous and next, and links from a report to the Qur'anic verses it quotes.
  • Historical context in depth: named eras, dated and cited events, and the places where narrators lived.

Goals

  • Curation, not broadcast. The archive presents evidence and attributed scholarship. It does not issue rulings, and no computed score is presented as a verdict.
  • Anti-sectarian. Every tradition's sources and grading schemes are first-class. Nothing defaults to one tradition.
  • Cumulative scholarship. In the classical manner of commentary upon commentary, contemporary scholars add attributed, reviewed work that stays linked to what it builds on.
  • Open. The code is open source and the published data is meant to be exportable with attribution, within the licences of the sources.
  • Honest about gaps. What is unknown is shown as unknown, and this page lists its own gaps and how each was found or closed.

Schema

As built in PostgreSQL, counted from the Drizzle schema files: 41 tables, 6 views and 1 materialized view, in 10 groups. pipeline tables are loaded from the datasets. curation tables are edited by people in the app. operations tables record loads and audit. derived views are computed from the others. Contribution system

The Open Scholarly Contribution System plan (March 2026) describes contributor profiles, five roles, eight contribution types, a review lifecycle, endorsements, and bulk collection submissions. It was written for Neo4j. Today only named curators can write: they sign in by name, every write is audited, and content moves through the states proposed, verified and published, with refused actions returned as plain results. Contributor accounts, submissions, peer review and endorsements are planned.

How publishing would work. A submission holds proposed content. When an editor publishes it, the content is written into the table where that kind of scholarship already lives (a commentary into commentaries, a narrator evaluation into narrator_assessments, a correction as a cited claim), and the audit record names the contributor and the submission. Readers query the same tables either way.

An open naming question. The glossary notes that the classical ijaza licenses a student to transmit, whereas the planned endorsement vouches for a contributor's expertise in a field. Whether the word ijaza should be used for it is undecided.

Not yet specified. Email registration and verification, account recovery, spam and abuse controls, personal-data handling, and a contributor licence compatible with the share-alike terms on data derived from OpenITI. Contributions cannot be published until the last of these is settled.

Where the plan and the current design differ. The plan's "graph-native" principle is kept in substance: every relationship is its own table with its own attributes. Its data exports as a Neo4j dump become a database dump plus CSV. Its figures (about 34,500 hadiths from six collections) predate the four-tradition corpus. And where older product notes describe an automatically calculated grade, the design shows chain facts and attributed verdicts only.

Dataset limitations

Figures are the most recent recorded: the load reports and diagnostic reports of 21 November 2026, the rights register, and an independent two-sided review of the same date. The earlier audit of 21 July 2026 is superseded where the two differ. A figure that appears in two documents with different values is given once, with the newer value: for example, the count of reports with a fully resolved chain is 142 after the empty-chain correction, where earlier reports gave 569 and the July audit gave 204. A figure that no document records is marked "not recorded". Each row says whether the limitation is open, partly closed or closed, and what closed it or what remains.
